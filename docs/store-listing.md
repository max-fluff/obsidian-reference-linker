# Community listing

What goes on the plugin's page in the community catalog. Kept here so the next release can
diff it instead of guessing what is live. The short description must match `manifest.json`
and the entry in `obsidianmd/obsidian-releases`.

## Short

Autocomplete links to documents outside your vault — PDF, Office, EPUB, images, recordings — that open at the exact page, and read them in the note itself: hover a link for the page, or embed it as a viewer you can page, zoom and play.

## Long

Your reference material — papers, manuals, decks, spreadsheets, e-books, recordings — usually lives outside the vault, in project folders, download folders and a research library. Reference Linker makes it as linkable as a note, and as readable as one.

**Link to a page, not a file.** Type the trigger and a document name. Where a format carries an outline — a PDF's bookmarks, Word and HTML headings, a workbook's sheets, an EPUB's table of contents, one entry per slide — every section is indexed on its page, so a query finds the *Introduction* of a paper rather than the paper. The link that lands in your note opens the document there, in your default app.

**Read it without leaving the note.** Hover a link for the page rendered, the slide drawn, the document laid out or the sheet as a real table. A `reference-link` block puts the same thing inline as a small viewer: page through it, zoom it, jump by its contents, or stack a whole range of pages and let each draw as you scroll to it. A recording gets its own transport — play, seek, volume — and can remember the second and the level you left it at.

**11 formats, 53 extensions, no converters.** PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, OpenDocument, EPUB, HTML, Markdown, CSV, images, audio and video. Every reader is the plugin's own: no pandoc, no LibreOffice, nothing to install alongside it.

**Links that notice the document changed.** A link pinned to its section is checked against the index. When the file is reissued and the section moves, the link is marked and repaired in place — one note or the whole vault, with a preview before anything is written.

**Nothing is copied in.** Notes keep a portable `{ref-root}` path, so the same vault works on another machine that keeps its library somewhere else.

Desktop only: it reads files from disk through Node's filesystem API, which isn't available on mobile.
